Juno Health

A preventive healthcare platform that alerts people early to diabetes, obesity and heart risks, and gives them personalised exercise plans, meal plans and health insights. I built most of the software behind it, and made sure the AI never answered without being checked.

The problem

Conditions such as diabetes, obesity and heart disease are far easier to manage when they are caught early, yet most people only see a doctor once symptoms appear. Juno Health set out to spot the warning signs sooner and to turn each person's health data into practical guidance they would actually follow.

Generative AI made the personalised guidance possible. It also created the main engineering risk: a language model can produce advice that sounds confident and is wrong for a particular patient. In healthcare, that is not acceptable.

The approach

One Node.js backend sits at the centre and serves every client: the Flutter mobile app, a web app for doctors and the public website. AI features run through that backend rather than from the apps directly, so every response from the OpenAI and Claude APIs passes the same gate: it is validated against the patient's profile before anyone sees it.

Simplified architecture. AI output flows back through the validation gate before reaching any app.

Results and lessons

  • 1,000+ downloads and 3 business clients, served by one backend.
  • One gateway for AI. Routing every model call through the backend meant tuning, validation and provider changes happen in one place, invisible to the apps.
  • Validation is a feature, not overhead. Checking AI output against the patient profile is what makes LLM-generated health guidance something a business client can put in front of its users.

The same pattern applies well beyond healthcare: wherever an LLM's answer has consequences, put a deterministic check between the model and the user.
